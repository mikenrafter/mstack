# Capture–recapture for review and reinspection

Research note, 2026-11-17. Literature and public-data discovery only; no new model fitting or review runs.

Capture–recapture is a closer conceptual match to the owner's question than Elo: it estimates defects that reviewers collectively missed, which can inform a reinspection decision. Published software studies exist. They do **not** establish that this works for sequential LLM reviews after repairs, or that a residual-defect estimate identifies the best number of extra review rounds.

## What the primary studies establish

**Briand, El Emam, Freimut and Laitenberger, TSE 2000**, DOI 10.1109/32.852741. This empirical evaluation used NASA/GSFC professionals inspecting requirements artifacts in the 1994/1995 reading experiments. Detection occurred without an intervening correction/inspection meeting. The final paper describes faults seeded or reintroduced before inspection, with known reference lists and adjudicated matching. It reports substantial underestimation with too few inspectors; below four, no estimator met its chosen ±20% relative-error criterion. Its recommendation favored a heterogeneity model with Jackknife estimation. This is evidence about those inspection conditions and that accuracy criterion, not a universal four-reviewer rule. The authors explicitly discuss differing defect detectability and limitations when reviewers specialize by defect type. **Evidence: empirical, controlled artifacts; transfer limited.** [Author-uploaded full paper, including NRC ERB-1084 reprint](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/3188084_A_comprehensive_evaluation_of_capture-recapture_models_for_estimating_software_defect_content).

**El Emam and Laitenberger, TSE September 2001**, DOI 10.1109/32.950319. The accessible NRC ERB-1068 preprint is dated December 1999. It evaluates six two-inspector estimators by Monte Carlo simulation: 48 conditions, 1,000 simulated inspections per condition, 30 faults, varying inspector capability and defect difficulty. It evaluates reinspection decisions as well as estimation error, recommending the Chapman-equivalent Mt(Ch) estimator under the studied conditions. That estimator permits different inspector abilities but assumes homogeneous defect detectability. Low overlap and substantial defect heterogeneity remain problematic. This is a simulated decision study, not evidence that two current LLM reviewers reliably estimate residual code defects. **Evidence: simulation, explicit assumptions.** [Full primary report](https://www.ehealthinformation.ca/web/default/files/wp-files/1068.pdf), [final publication](https://doi.org/10.1109/32.950319).

**Walia, Carver and Nagappan, ICSE May 2008**, DOI 10.1145/1368088.1368134. The institutional abstract reports data from 73 inspectors and investigates the number needed to estimate within 5–20% of the reference count. Sample-coverage estimators outperformed Jackknife in that study, challenging a universal estimator preference. I located the primary abstract, not the full ICSE paper; do not infer undocumented methods or uncertainty from it. A related author-published ISSRE 2008 paper describes Microsoft professionals inspecting a financial requirements document with 30 seeded realistic faults; its additional experimental conditions must not be silently attributed to the ICSE paper. **Evidence: empirical abstract verified; full ICSE methods unavailable here.** [Institutional primary record and abstract](https://augusta.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/the-effect-of-the-number-of-inspectors-on-the-defect-estimates-pr/), [related ISSRE paper](https://carver.cs.ua.edu/Papers/Conference/2008/2008_ISSRE.pdf).

**Barnard, El Emam and Zubrow, SEI/United Space Alliance, April 12, 2002.** This industrial design/code-inspection presentation examines 861 inspections across seven releases over approximately seven years. Of 308 inspections with defects, filtering out zero/one-finding inspections and reinspections left 89 eligible cases. MtChao produced estimates in 85; the deck reports 64% decision accuracy and 80% with an augmented logistic model. Different tables use different denominators; these are not results across all 861 inspections. Older releases had longer downstream exposure. The slides do not establish an independent prospective validation or causal benefit from allocating extra reviews. Their exclusion of sparse inspections particularly limits transfer to late, mostly clean reviews. **Evidence: industrial retrospective presentation; selection and follow-up limitations.** [SEI primary slides](https://insights.sei.cmu.edu/documents/2935/2002_017_001_22643.pdf).

**Walia and Carver, ESEM October 2008**, DOI 10.1145/1414004.1414031. Four student capstone requirements artifacts were inspected by their own developer teams of 8, 8, 6 and 6. The documents remained unchanged between two inspections, with additional training between them. The reference count was the union of defects found in both inspections; the authors explicitly acknowledge unknown actual defect totals. Estimates improved relative to this incomplete reference after the second inspection. This supports studying unchanged artifacts and documenting reference limitations; it does not demonstrate that all remaining defects were measured. **Evidence: four natural-artifact cases, incomplete reference truth.** [Primary author version, sections 4.2–4.4](https://carver.cs.ua.edu/papers/conference/2008/2008_esem_authorversion.pdf).

## The usable mathematical object

For two inspectors let n₁ and n₂ be their accepted unique defect counts and m their accepted overlap. The Chapman estimate is

`N̂ = ((n₁ + 1)(n₂ + 1)/(m + 1)) − 1`;

observed union is `D = n₁ + n₂ − m`, and estimated unseen defects are `N̂ − D`. This finite correction does not make zero-overlap cases informative or guarantee a useful uncertainty interval. [El Emam and Laitenberger, estimator discussion](https://www.ehealthinformation.ca/web/default/files/wp-files/1068.pdf).

Our design inference: the essential input is an inspector-by-defect incidence matrix for the **same frozen artifact and scope**, with independent private detection and reliable defect identity/adjudication. A shared easy/hard fault structure can make two otherwise independent reviewers overlap disproportionately on easy faults. Positive reviewer dependence can similarly inflate overlap and underestimate the unseen population. Different prompting perspectives may change those assumptions rather than repair them automatically.

Existing changed-code/diff-only rounds generally fail this sampling definition: repaired defects disappear, new defects can arise, scopes differ, and prior findings can direct later attention. Their lack of recapture is therefore not ordinary independent failure to detect the same fixed defect population. Do not apply the closed-population formula to the current round ledger without proving eligibility. No located study validates the transfer to LLM review.

Even a valid residual-count estimator needs a separate decision model: severity, next-review detectability, repair success, induced regressions and review cost. Count-estimation accuracy and extra-review utility are different outcomes.

## Public original data found

The [UMD PBR laboratory package](https://www.cs.umd.edu/projects/SoftEng/ESEG/manual/pbr_package/manual.html) links a [computation page](https://www.cs.umd.edu/projects/SoftEng/ESEG/manual/pbr_package/node57.html) and an openly downloadable [C++ source containing eight participant-by-reference-fault matrices](https://www.cs.umd.edu/projects/SoftEng/ESEG/manual/pbr_package/docs/total.cc). The unchanged source is saved locally with SHA-256 and a parser map in [SOURCE.json](sources/SOURCE.json). **It has not been compiled or executed.** Treat it as published data; do not run its legacy program.

The [original PBR study](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~mvz/handouts/emp_pbr.pdf) describes November 1994 and June 1995 cohorts, professional participants, usual versus perspective-based reading, and document revisions between replications. Participant IDs recur across different documents within each cohort. Cross-cohort personal identity is not established here. Documents, reference-fault identities and cohorts cannot be pooled as interchangeable observations. The study discusses reference-list reconciliation; exact correspondence of this downloadable matrix version to every final-paper analysis is not verified. These are known study faults, not an exhaustive census of all natural defects. An empirical diagnostic may compare estimates with the matrix's reference columns, explicitly calling this **reference-fault recovery**.

The package's [download page](https://www.cs.umd.edu/projects/SoftEng/ESEG/manual/pbr_package/download.html) indicates defect descriptions were removed for teaching use. No request or other outreach was made. Public matrix availability does not supply defect semantics, severity, modern code-review validity or independent deployment outcomes.
